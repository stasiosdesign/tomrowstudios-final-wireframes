Making the product clear in practice

Rayon needed a way to show architects and interior designers exactly how the software works. Not through abstract promises or feature lists. Through real demonstration. The brief was straightforward. Create a series of tutorial films that communicate the tool's capabilities with precision and clarity.

Tom Rowntree managed the full production process from concept through to final delivery. This began with planning the tutorial structure. Each film was designed to address a specific workflow. Drawing a floor plan. Sharing a file with a collaborator. Building a library of reusable details. The goal was never to impress. It was to instruct.

Filming took place in working architectural studios. This was a deliberate choice. The software needed to be seen in the environment where it would actually be used. Not in a sterile showroom. The camera captured real screens, real hands, and real design decisions as they happened. The result is content that feels honest and immediately useful.

Editing focused on removing anything that did not serve the lesson. No music. No slow-motion pans across empty desks. Just the software interface, a clear voiceover, and the steps an architect needs to follow. Each tutorial runs long enough to teach the skill and short enough to watch between meetings.

The finished films show Rayon functioning inside genuine design situations. An architect drawing over a scanned plan. A team commenting on a shared layout. A designer pulling together a mood board directly within the tool. The tutorials do not tell people the product works. They watch it work.
